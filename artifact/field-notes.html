Field notes

What the room taught

  1. The video prize judges the film, so the film can be about anything you built.

    HyperFrames makes video from HTML. Its own creative team uses it where they used to open After Effects. The project in the film does not have to use HyperFrames; only the film does.

  2. Talk to it like a director.

    The advice from the HyperFrames table: the more film vocabulary you bring (shots, cuts, pacing), the better the result. Creative people get the most out of it. Their launch film is the reference for what good looks like.

  3. The word “hackathon” does not travel well.

    MLH grew up with students and is moving toward working professionals who need to learn AI. In some cities the public hears “hackathon” and thinks of break-ins, so the name itself gets tested.

  4. A dossier is a good test of a software factory.

    One challenge on the floor: give the system a company name, get back a short dossier with the chief executive and five or six facts. The hard part is how you arrange the prompts and roles so the result is reliably good. John’s own factory needs the same work.

  5. For a solo operator, taste is the only thing that sets the work apart.

    That is why the notes become a film. John is moving from an audio podcast to video, and wants bumpers and chapter cards across his whole library. This hackathon is the first one made that way.
